# Thermal coupling survey, 2026-09-18

The evidence base for roadmap revision 6: the revision that adds the part-to-part, water and fire lanes and
makes the existing point-wise machinery reachable from a scene config. Produced by two web-research sweeps
and three repository audits run in parallel on 2026-09-18, then judged and adversarially verified;
**65 sourced findings** (52 high-confidence: page or PDF fetched and read), **48 audit findings**
with file:line evidence, and 24 open-source codes with licences.

Companion to [`2026-09-15-field-survey.md`](2026-09-15-field-survey.md), which it does not repeat: that survey
covered MuSES mesh conduction, the DIRSIG THERM limits, the Fraunhofer IOSB prisms, FTOM-2D, VIRSuite and
ThRend. This one covers what the owner asked for on 2026-09-18 and the earlier survey did not reach —
conduction **between parts**, the engine as a solved node, hot soak, shadows and sky view from real geometry,
the latent-heat term, water bodies, fire and plumes as participating media, and snow, vegetation and people.

This file is the citation list the roadmap's step rows point at. It is a snapshot of what was published and
readable on 2026-09-18, not a maintained document: check a source before relying on it, and prefer the
primary link over this summary. Numbers quoted here are **external evidence**, not irsim measurements
(risk R13); a roadmap row that adopts one as a tolerance says so.

## What the repository audit found

Three read-only audits over the tree at commit `7f0b372` (clean, `make check` green: lint, mypy over 187
files, 2 985 + 251 tests). Severity is the auditor's; every finding names the file and line it rests on.

### The thermal core and the Isaac glue

The engine-free core has real, well-tested pieces of R1 — a PlanarPatch/PlanarThermalField that solves N independent §6.1 cells, an exact ray-rectangle per-cell shadow (shadow.py), and a computed parallel-rectangle view factor (spatial_sources.py) — but none of them is reachable from a scene YAML: `patch:` blocks exist in the schema and in zero shipped scenes, `_build_thermal_field` still solves a patched surface as ONE facet, PatchSpec has no occluder field, `cell_shadow`/`patch_solar_loading` have no caller outside tests, and the only bound fields (car bonnet, road) are hand-built in Python by `car_demo.build_car_demo`, which passes no solar term at all. So "a building half in sun from a config plus one command" cannot be produced today; the wall exists only as a unit test, and shadows come from authored ShadowRectangles, never from the object's own mesh. R2 is essentially absent: `FacetSolver.net_flux` (facets.py:146-158) has no inter-cell term, there is no node-graph/conductance solver anywhere, the engine/exhaust/underbody are independent scripted first-order rows whose coupling to each other is an author-chosen load fraction, and the two conduction-carrying objects that do exist (LumpedTwoNodeSolver: through-thickness only; CabinNode: panel-to-air) have zero callers and no `solver:` kind. Engine-bay convection onto the bonnet underside is not modelled — it is "absorbed" into a 0.95 cavity emissivity. R3 is thinner still: water is the open-sea skin model only (cool skin + warm layer on an authored bulk SST as a background profile), with no latent-heat/evaporation term in any balance, no consumer of `precip_mm_h`, wetness as a separate material rather than a state; fire, flame, plume, participating media and gas emission have no code at all (plume is "deferred without a number"; LUT tops out at 1000 K), and the point-target module only offers a grey sub-pixel radiance injection. Structurally, the fixed explicit 1 s/60 s tick with no stability guard and an unbounded tick history (PT.8) cannot host an implicit conduction operator as-is, the PT.6/PT.7 demo constants bypass the material library and start every field uniform, and the per-pixel path is host-side NumPy keyed by instance id + planar patch (curved geometry stays per-prim until the unshipped WM lane). Several docstrings, the CHANGELOG and the roadmap describe PT.2 as making point-wise scenes declarable and consumed by a render driver; no driver reads `Scene.patches`, so those claims are ahead of the code. For R4 the honest state is: point-wise machinery exists and is correct where tested, but the owner's three headline phenomena are reachable in one Python-authored night car-park scene only, and R2/R3 have no design surface yet.

### The roadmap

The roadmap (docs/roadmap.md, 112 parsed step rows: 84 open, 28 done; queue machinery sound, `--check` green, every dep id resolves) is a well-built plan for an aerial-then-maritime IR camera, but as written it does not lead to R1-R3. R1 (per-point sun/shadow across one object) has real engine-free machinery (PT.1 shadow, PT.2 PatchSpec, PT.5 local frames) yet none of it is reachable from a scene config: no scene YAML carries `patches:`, the schema has no occluder field, `ShadowRectangle`/`cell_shadow` have no caller outside `irsim.thermal.__init__`, and the only bound fields are hand-built in `car_demo.py`; the sole binding steps are PT.9 (aerial, phase A but dependent on phase-B WM.3, creating a phase-exit circularity the prose at line 229 contradicts) and PT.10 (maritime); no step ever produces a building/wall/roof scene, and line 605 explicitly defers building envelopes and "the solver's missing occlusion input" to phase C. Shadows from an object's own geometry arrive only with WM.4 (phase B, queue position 14, four steps deep) and that step is self-shadowing per prim, so a neighbouring building or tree still cannot cast a shadow. R2 (heat spreading engine -> mounts -> chassis -> panels) has no step at all: the engine is a scripted ΔT-over-ambient schedule (ADR 0089, vehicle.py:1-7), its only coupling to panels is a parallel-rectangle radiative view factor (spatial_sources.py), there is no node-to-node conductance anywhere in the plan, and the nearest steps (PT.11 lateral conduction within one patch, PT.12, PT.15) are all phase C, PT.11 size L at queue position 16 under a phase whose header says "deferred breadth stays deferred". R3 is almost entirely absent: water exists only as the sea, §6.1 has no latent-heat term so evaporation/wet-surface cooling cannot emerge (the spec's own Tier 3 "wet asphalt reads colder" item is unproducible), the weather file's `precip_mm_h` column is read by nothing, fire and hot gas appear nowhere, and the exhaust-plume deferral's revisit trigger ("MWIR Tier 3") has already been met by the shipped MWIR config and `test_tier3_multiband.py` without the row changing; snow, vegetation and humans are material YAMLs only. R4: the machinery is trustworthy, but the phase-plan table drifts from the rows in seven places, ten open verification cells state motivation rather than what would fail, the document calls itself both Revision 4 and Revision 5 and claims 109 steps against 112, and the WM-behind-phase-A rationale (four CPU defects) has expired because all four are done. The plan needs roughly five new rows and three re-phasings before it can honestly be said to converge on the owner's camera: a ground per-point binding step that reads `Scene.patches` plus an occluder block, a building scene config, a thermal-network (node + conductance) step with the engine as a solved node, a latent-heat/wet-surface step, and a numbered plume/fire decision, with PT.11 and the network step pulled out of phase C.

## Research findings by topic

### Part-to-part heat transfer: how production codes link parts

9 findings.

- **(a) heat-source specification in MuSES.** MuSES groups geometry into parts (one material/thermal property set per part), places several thermal nodes through each element's thickness, and its user-routine API sets per node any of: temperature, convection coefficient, fluid temperature, imposed heat, capacitance, area, emissivity, absorptivity; it also exposes engine power and speed as scenario inputs and returns per-node conduction/convection/radiation/advection heat rates. Restarts recompute 'view factor and conductor calculations' and require the same number and assignment of thermal nodes.  
  *source:* Curran & Gonda, Applications of the MuSES Infrared Signature Code, NATO RTO-MP-SCI-145 paper 14 — https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/RTO-MP-SCI-145/MP-SCI-145-14.pdf  
  *quote:* "Retrieve and Set the temperature, convection coefficient, fluid temperature, imposed heat, capacitance, area, emissivity, or absorptivity of a node."  
  *for irsim:* R2/R4. irsim.thermal.vehicle and irsim.thermal.aerial only have prescribed-TEMPERATURE nodes. A new engine-free module irsim/thermal/network.py should define a node with capacity C and exactly the three production boundary kinds: fixed temperature, imposed heat Q [W], and convection to a fluid node (h*A, T_fluid), plus conductors G [W/K] between nodes. Engine heat then enters as watts = engine_power * loss_fraction (from VehicleState), not as a scripted skin temperature. Test that would fail if wrong: an imposed-heat node with conductance G to a fixed-temperature sink must settle at dT = Q/G to 1e-6 relative, and switching the same source to a fluid-convection kind must give dT = Q/(hA) - a solver that treats all sources as temperatures cannot pass either.
- **(a) conductor/contactor taxonomy in SINDA/Thermal Desktop.** Thermal Desktop defines part-to-part conduction as: edge contact, area contact, node-node conductors on the same or different objects, finite-element conductance, user conductors, and 'contactors' that contact a group of surfaces to another group with a user tolerance and integration level; nodes do not need to be aligned; like conductors are summed before solve. Heat loads apply to surfaces or nodes as flux, time-dependent or area-distributed; heaters take input power and on/off temperatures. RadCAD view factors are Monte Carlo: F_ij = rays from i hitting j / rays shot, 5-20k rays for final runs.  
  *source:* Introduction to Thermal Desktop, TFAWS 2003 training (C&R Technologies) — https://tfaws.nasa.gov/TFAWS03/software_training/thermal_desktop.pdf  
  *quote:* "Contact a group of surfaces to another group of surfaces • User controlled tolerance and integration levels ... Nodes do not need to be aligned"  
  *for irsim:* R2. - **Fire: how FDS (open source, NIST) renders flame radiation - the slab kernel.** FDS solves a gray-gas radiative transfer equation with an absorption coefficient from the RadCal narrow-band model because 'in real fires, soot is the dominant source and sink of thermal radiation'; for optically thin flames it uses six spectral bands for fuel, CO2 and H2O; the radiative fraction chi_r is 0.3-0.4 for most combustibles; and its band absorption coefficients are defined by a uniform-slab identity: the band-integrated intensity leaving a layer of thickness L equals (sigma/pi)[(1 - e^{-kappa L}) T^4 + e^{-kappa L} T_rad^4].   *quote:* "it is usually not necessary because in real fires, soot is the dominant source and sink of thermal radiation and is not particulary sensitive to wavelength. C.3 read as a radiance-space operator. Implement it as irsim/pipeline/gas_slab.py next to point_target.py (MS.6) with kappa_b(T) as a small tabulated per-band asset generated by `make luts` (bands stay data, CLAUDE.md 'bands are data'). - **Fire: solid-flame model, soot emissivity vs thickness, gas emissivity (Hottel form) and view factor.** For a flame of mean beam length L_ms and extinction coefficient K, the emissivity is E = 1 - exp(-K L_ms) and the emitted flux q = (1 - exp(-beta D)) sigma T_f^4, 'optically thick' when beta D is large; soot emits a continuum while CO2 and H2O are 'the most important emitting gases' with E = E_CO2 + E_H2O - dE (chart-based overlap correction); for hydrocarbons the unobscured flame's surface emissive power is 100-170 kW/m2 (black-body 1150-1300 K) and a smoke-obscured 'blooming' flame 30-50 kW/m2; radiation received by a target is q_t = tau_atm * SEP * F_t-s, with the view factor defined as the fraction of radiation leaving one surface that is incident on the other.

  *source:* RADIS GitHub repository README — https://github.com/radis/radis  
  *quote:* "a fast line-by-line code for high resolution infrared molecular spectra (emission / absorption, equilibrium / non-LTE)"  
  *for irsim:* The offline generator for data/ hot-gas band tables: scripts/generate_gas_luts.py runs RADIS over 300-2500 K for CO2 and H2O at 1 atm, integrates against each configured band's spectral response (irsim.radiometry.spectral_response) and writes float32 kappa_b(T) tables, exactly as `make luts` does for Planck. RADIS stays a scripts/ dependency; test_layering.py already forbids it in src/irsim, and a small golden test pins one table value (e.g. CO2 band-mean absorption in 3-5 um at 1200 K) so a regeneration with the ambient HITRAN database is caught.

### Exhaust and steam plumes, and what each band sees

3 findings.

- **(R3) volumetric plumes in production IR simulators.** DIRSIG5 generates plumes externally (enhanced Blackadar puff model) into OpenVDB voxel files read by a PlumeVDB plugin; ThermoAnalytics voxelises the CFD plume (pressure, temperature, H2O/CO2/soot concentrations) around the hardbody as a participating medium that attenuates or adds to the signal between sensor and target, with MODTRAN path radiance.  
  *source:* DIRSIG Blackadar Plume Model / PlumeVDB plugin docs; ThermoAnalytics Plume Radiation & Signature — https://www.thermoanalytics.com/application/plume-radiation-signature/  
  *quote:* "voxelizing the plume volume around the hardbody"  
  *for irsim:* R3. Fire/hot gas is a volumetric participating medium, not a surface: the engine-free step is a voxel grid of (T, species column) sampled along each pixel ray with band-averaged gas absorption (H2O/CO2), added to irsim.pipeline before atmosphere. The survey already notes NanoVDB/Warp volume sampling for the Isaac side. Test: a homogeneous isothermal slab of gas must reproduce L = eps_gas(T,tau) * L_bb(T) + (1-eps_gas) * L_behind for the analytic band emissivity, and go to zero as the column goes to zero.
- **Exhaust plumes: NIRATAM species and MWIR vs LWIR transmission (NATO).** NATO's NIRATAM v3.1 with the NPLUME v1.6 flow-field code accounts for plume radiation only from CO, CO2, H2O and soot; fitting measured ship-plume spectra, the plume's average transmission is 65 % near the exit plane rising to 100 % where it dissolves in the 3.0-5.0 um band, but 90-100 % in the 8.0-10.0 um band; the active species are the same as in the intervening atmosphere, with deeper and wider absorption features in the plume.  
  *source:* Bakker et al. (TNO), Modeling multi-spectral imagery data with NIRATAM v3.1 and NPLUME v1.6, Proc. SPIE 3699 (1999) — https://publications.tno.nl/publication/34607494/eyWnwu/bakker-1999-modeling.pdf  
  *quote:* "Since NIRATAM only takes into account plume radiation from CO, CO2, H2O, and soot, the analysis is necessarily limited to these species ... The average transmission through the plume in the mid-wave infrared (3.0 to 5.0 um) ranges from 65 % close to the exit plane, to 100 % where the plume dissolves in the ambient atmosphere. For the long-wave infrared (8.0 to 10.0 um) the range in transmission is 90 % to 100 %."  
  *for irsim:* For the maritime lane (vessel funnel) and the car demo (MP.4b exhaust), an exhaust plume is a gas slab that is nearly transparent in LWIR (<=10 % absorption -> a few-K apparent contrast from a 500 K gas) and the dominant MWIR feature. The would-fail test: a plume slab with authored T, p_CO2, p_H2O and L must give tau_LWIR >= 0.90 and tau_MWIR in 0.65-1.0 decreasing toward the exit; if the slab were grey the two would be equal and the test fails. This is also why a bolometer-only project can defer plume work behind the hot exhaust surface it already has.
- **Steam and water-droplet plumes (vs gas plumes).** Condensed steam is a droplet cloud, not a gas: FLIR's fire-test note attributes LWIR's ability to image through 'thick layers of smoke and steam' to wavelengths larger than the steam droplets, while the MWIR camera needed a narrowband filter and still lost the scene; NIRATAM shows the gaseous H2O/CO2 of a plume transmits 90-100 % in 8-10 um, so what a LWIR camera sees of a steam plume is the droplet phase (emitting near the droplet temperature, cooling from ~100 C at the exit to ambient) and it fades as the droplets evaporate.  
  *source:* Application of Mid-Wave and Long-Wave IR Imaging in Large-Scale Rack-Storage Fires (Teledyne FLIR) and Bakker et al. 1999 (TNO/NIRATAM) — https://www.infraredtraining.com/en-US/home/resources/blog/application-of-mid-wave-and-long-wave-ir-imaging--in-large-scale-rack-storage-fires/  
  *quote:* "clearly showing the fire location and suppression behavior of the water through thick layers of smoke and steam ... Long-wave infrared utilizes wavelengths larger than the size of steam droplets or soot particles, resulted in great signal transmittance"  
  *for irsim:* Steam/vapour is the same slab operator as fire but with a droplet extinction (Mie, scaled by liquid water content, as irsim/atmosphere/cloud.py and extinction.py already do for fog/cloud) rather than a gas kappa_b(T), emitting at the droplet temperature. Tests that fail if the physics is wrong: for the same LWC the MWIR extinction must exceed the LWIR extinction (band ordering), the apparent temperature of the plume must never exceed the authored droplet temperature, and a pure-gas H2O slab at 373 K must be nearly invisible in LWIR (tau >= 0.9 per NIRATAM). Rotor-veil injection (rotor_veil.py) is the existing precedent for a translucent overlay in the pipeline.

### Fire on a real camera: gain states, saturation and AGC

2 findings.

- **Fire on a LWIR microbolometer: gain states, saturation, AGC.** The FLIR Boson provides high-gain and low-gain states: in high gain 'hot objects in the scene are prone to saturation' while in low gain 'scene range is significantly higher'; intrascene temperature is the span that maps to the 16-bit output without railing, and radiometric cameras' output is not capped; the AGC is a variant of histogram equalisation in which 'an image with 60 % sky will devote 60 % of the available 8-bit values ... to the sky', with plateau, tail-rejection, max-gain and linear-percent parameters. FLIR product pages give the ranges as up to 140 C (high gain) and 500 C (low gain).  
  *source:* FLIR Boson Thermal Imaging Core datasheet, Doc. 102-2013-40 Rev 340 (March 2021), sections 6.5, 6.8, 11.2 — https://groupgets-files.s3.amazonaws.com/boson/documents/Boson%20datasheet,%20102-2013-40,%20Rev%20340.pdf  
  *quote:* "In high-gain state, the thermal sensitivity is highest (i.e., NEDT is lowest), but hot objects in the scene are prone to saturation. In low-gain state, the thermal sensitivity is lower, but scene range is significantly higher ... In classic HEQ, an image with 60% sky will devote 60% of the available 8-bit values (referred to as grayshades here forward) to the sky"  
  *for irsim:* The camera the project validates against (configs/sensors/halmstad_boson_320.yaml) needs a gain_state with an intrascene ceiling so a fire rails the 16-bit signal in high gain and merely compresses it in low gain; the bolometer.py 'un-clipped signal' must be clipped at the ceiling before the ISP. Test in the test_agc_collapse.py family: a 1000 K slab covering 2 % of pixels over a 300 K scene must, under agc_plateau, leave the background at least N grayshades (plateau-limited), whereas agc_linear collapses it - and a radiometric config must keep the true temperature above the 8-bit ceiling (FLAME 3's 500 C cap is the reference behaviour).
- **Fire on a LWIR camera: linear AGC collapse quantified (FLIR).** FLIR's own AGC note: with a 37 C human in a 30 C room and a 1000 C fire, under linear AGC 'the 7 degrees Celsius between the human and the room won't show in the image as visible contrast, since it represents only 0.7% of the total range'; capping the range at 100 C makes 'everything hotter than 100 C look the same'; histogram equalisation (CLAHE in the example) recovers both.  
  *source:* Basic AGC for radiometric Lepton images, Teledyne FLIR OEM — https://oem.flir.com/learn/discover/basic-agc-for-radiometric-lepton-images/  
  *quote:* "the 7 degrees Celsius between the human and the room won't show in the image as visible contrast, since it represents only 0.7% of the total range"  
  *for irsim:* A numeric acceptance test for irsim.isp.agc under fire: render (or synthesise via conftest fixtures) a frame with a 303 K background, a 310 K person and a 1273 K fire patch; assert agc_linear yields < 2 DN person/room contrast (0.7 % of 255) and agc_plateau yields >= 10 DN. Also a reminder for R4 ('a real infrared camera'): the delivered 8-bit frame of a fire scene is dominated by the AGC choice, so the config must record which AGC mode the reference camera used.

### Water bodies, wet surfaces and evaporation

9 findings.

- **(R3) wet surfaces and evaporative cooling.** Hendel's cool-pavement review formalises the surface balance with a latent term (evaporation rate x latent heat) for a water film whose evaporation rate rises with film temperature and depends on ambient vapour pressure; reviews of pavement watering report surface-temperature reductions of 10-15 C during insolation (Hendel et al. 2014, 2016) and up to ~40 C for water-retaining pavements, detected by infrared camera as temperature spikes when the surface dries. *(medium confidence)*  
  *source:* Hendel, Cool pavements (arXiv 2409.12242); Thermal performance of cooling strategies for asphalt pavement: a state-of-the-art review — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12242  
  *quote:* "the evaporation rate increases with the water film temperature"  
  *for irsim:* R3. Add a water-film state and latent flux E*l to irsim.thermal.balance (FacetProperties gains wetness_kg_m2; forcing gains vapour pressure from WeatherSample humidity, which WeatherSeries already carries per non-negotiable #6). Test: a wetted asphalt facet at solar noon is >=10 K cooler than the dry twin and rejoins it once the film mass reaches zero (fails if latent term or film depletion is missing).
- **Wet surfaces: the latent-heat term (Penman-Monteith, FAO-56).** The Penman-Monteith combination equation gives latent heat flux from net radiation Rn, soil heat flux G, vapour pressure deficit (es - ea), air density and cp, the saturation-curve slope, the psychrometric constant and the bulk surface and aerodynamic resistances rs and ra; ra = ln((zm - d)/zom) ln((zh - d)/zoh) / (k^2 uz) with von Karman k = 0.41; the latent heat of vaporisation used is 2.45 MJ/kg.  
  *source:* FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56, Chapter 2: FAO Penman-Monteith equation — https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e06.htm  
  *quote:* "where Rn is the net radiation, G is the soil heat flux, (es - ea) represents the vapour pressure deficit of the air, ra is the mean air density at constant pressure, cp is the specific heat of the air, D represents the slope of the saturation vapour pressure temperature relationship, g is the psychrometric constant, and rs and ra are the (bulk) surface and aerodynamic resistances"  
  *for irsim:* The missing term in balance.py's sec. 6.1 equation is -lambda E. For a free water film rs = 0 and Penman-Monteith reduces to the bulk form E = rho_a (q_sat(T_s) - q_a)/ra, so one implementation covers wet pavement (rs = 0), vegetation (rs = stomatal) and bare soil (rs from moisture). q_sat comes from irsim.atmosphere.humidity (vapour_pressure_hpa already exists), lambda = 2.45e6 J/kg goes into irsim.radiometry.constants with this citation. Test that fails if wrong: a saturated cell with no radiation and h > 0 relaxes to the psychrometric wet-bulb temperature of (T_air, RH), and its steady temperature decreases monotonically with decreasing RH; a dry cell (film mass 0) must reproduce today's result bit-for-bit.
- **Wet surfaces / water bodies: bulk aerodynamic flux formulae (AeroBulk / COARE).** AeroBulk implements the bulk formulae Q_H = rho C_H c_p (theta_z - T_s) U_B and E = rho C_E (q_s - q_z) U_B with Q_L = -L_v E, offering COARE v3.0/v3.6, ECMWF, ANDREAS and NCAR algorithms, with a cool-skin/warm-layer scheme available when the input is bulk SST; licence GPL-3.0.  
  *source:* AeroBulk: aerodynamic bulk formulae for air-sea turbulent fluxes (GitHub README) — https://github.com/brodeau/aerobulk  
  *quote:* "In the COARE and ECMWF algorithms, a cool-skin/warm layer scheme is included and can be activated if the input sea-surface temperature to be used is the bulk SST."  
  *for irsim:* sea_skin.py's docstring already admits the latent term is 'usually the largest of the three' and is missing. Add E = rho C_E U (q_sat(T_skin) - q_a) with C_E ~ C_H (Dalton number) to the sea and lake skin balance and to the wet-film cell; use AeroBulk (GPL, so oracle only, never vendored) to pin a golden value, e.g. Q_L for U = 5 m/s, T_s = 293 K, RH = 0.7 must land within 15 % of COARE 3.6. Because irsim's WeatherSample carries rh_fraction and wind, no schema change is needed; a test must assert that removing the latent term warms the sea skin (sign check) and that Q_L = 0 when RH = 1 and T_s = T_a.
- **Wet surfaces: how EnergyPlus models rain (the production shortcut).** EnergyPlus: 'When the outside environment indicates that it is raining, the exterior surfaces (exposed to wind) are assumed to be wet. The convection coefficient is set to a very high number (1000) and the outside temperature used for the surface will be the wet-bulb temperature.' No evaporation or latent heat is modelled on opaque exterior surfaces.  
  *source:* EnergyPlus 25.1 Engineering Reference: Outside Surface Heat Balance — https://bigladdersoftware.com/epx/docs/25-1/engineering-reference/outside-surface-heat-balance.html  
  *quote:* "When the outside environment indicates that it is raining, the exterior surfaces (exposed to wind) are assumed to be wet. The convection coefficient is set to a very high number (1000)"  
  *for irsim:* A one-commit ablation level for wet surfaces before the full film model (ADR 0083 pattern: fidelity switches are config): while precip_mm_h > 0, set h = 1000 W/m2/K and T_air := T_wb in FacetForcing. It is also a correctness check on the later latent term: with rs = 0 and large h the full model must converge to the same wet-bulb clamp. Test: rain on a facet gives |T_s - T_wb(T_air, RH)| < 0.1 K within one tick; and the WeatherSeries used by the atmosphere is the same object (non-negotiable #6), so a test asserts that the precip flag comes from the injected series, not a scene flag.
- **Wet surfaces: measured wet-vs-dry asphalt temperature difference and drying time.** Pavement watering in Paris (0.20 mm per application every 60 min in shade and every 30 min in sun, FLIR B400 camera) reduced asphalt surface temperature by up to 4 C during shading and 13 C during insolation; averaged over zones, 2-4 C in the morning and 6-13 C in the afternoon; the camera detected 'temperature spikes indicative of surface drying', i.e. the surface re-warms abruptly when the film is gone within the 30-60 min cycle.  
  *source:* Hendel, Colombert, Diab, Royon, Improving a pavement-watering method on the basis of pavement surface temperature measurements, Urban Climate (2014), arXiv 1412.2165 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.2165  
  *quote:* "surface temperature reductions of up to 4 C during shading and 13 C during insolation were found. Secondly, the infrared camera successfully detected temperature spikes indicative of surface drying"  
  *for irsim:* The Tier 3 phenomenology numbers for the wet-film cell: an asphalt PlanarPatch at noon, clear summer weather (existing clear_midlat_summer_48h), with a 0.2 mm film on half its cells must render the wet half 6-13 K colder than the dry half and 2-4 K in shade, and the film must evaporate in roughly 30-60 min (mass budget E dt vs 0.2 kg/m2) after which the two halves reconverge - the 'spike'. This is R1 in its purest form: wet and dry are per-cell states of one object, so the film mass must live on the cell like q_solar, not on the prim. A test that fails if the latent term is scaled wrong: drying time outside 15-120 min at the paper's conditions.
- **Wet surfaces: asphalt thermal behaviour dry vs saturated (Nottingham) and permeable-pavement cooling.** Hassn, Chiarelli, Dawson and Garcia (2016) exposed dry and water-saturated asphalt slabs to infrared light and measured surface/bottom temperature, heat flux and evaporation rate: under wet conditions 'the energy used by water for evaporation reduces drastically the temperature of asphalt mixture', whereas in dry conditions air voids act only through heat capacity and conductivity; related permeable-pavement studies report 5-15 C lower surface temperatures with evaporative cooling. *(medium confidence)*  
  *source:* Thermal properties of asphalt pavements under dry and wet conditions, Materials & Design (2016), Nottingham ePrints 35660; Li et al., Cooling effect of permeable asphalt pavement under dry and wet conditions — https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/35660/  
  *for irsim:* Second, independent magnitude (5-15 C) for the wet-asphalt test above; also a reminder that water in the pores changes the slab's k and rho c, so the wet state should raise the cell's heat_capacity_j_m2_k as well as add evaporation. Keep it as a documented tolerance, not a golden number, until the paper is read.
- **Inland water bodies: skin vs bulk temperature.** At Lake Tahoe the night-time skin effect had a mean of 0.3-0.5 K (skin cooler than bulk) and the daytime effect a mean of ~0.5 K in the morning falling to 0.1 K by midday; increased downwelling sky radiation from cloud or high humidity produced night-time skin temperatures warmer than bulk. Microprofiles on a stratified lake at 0.8-8.2 m/s winds show the diffusive thermal sublayer is 0.7-3.6 mm thick and natural convection sets its thickness and the skin-to-bulk difference. *(medium confidence)*  
  *source:* Wilson et al., Skin and bulk temperature difference at Lake Tahoe: a case study on lake skin effect, JGR Atmospheres (2013); Hondzo et al., Measurement and Scaling of Lake Surface Skin Temperatures, GRL (2022) — https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jgrd.50786  
  *quote:* "The diffusive thermal sublayer thickness ranged from 0.7 to 3.6 mm. The temperature microprofiles and proposed parameterization of skin-to-bulk temperatures enable similarity scaling"  
  *for irsim:* Lakes and rivers reuse sea_skin.py (cool_skin_thickness_m, cool_skin_deficit_k, warm_layer_k) with fresh-water constants and a much larger effective heat capacity than any solid cell, so their diurnal amplitude is small and lagged. Two tests that fail if the physics is wrong: (a) clear calm night gives skin - bulk in [-0.5, -0.1] K; (b) cloudy humid night (Q_LW_down high) flips the sign to skin warmer than bulk - a model that only ever cools the skin fails (b). The computed sublayer thickness at 0.8-8.2 m/s must lie in 0.7-3.6 mm.
- **Standing water and puddles: angular emissivity and reflected sky.** Surface-leaving radiance over water is R_nu(theta) = eps_nu(theta) B_nu(T_s) + r_nu(theta) I_nu_down with eps = 1 - r; the reflectivity comes from the Fresnel equations for a specular surface plus a wave-slope distribution; field spectra (M-AERI) show that the classic emissivity models 'are deficient at larger view angles and wind speeds', and the cross-over angle where roughness starts to raise emissivity is 30-40 degrees rather than the 68 degrees of Masuda et al. (1988), whose model tabulates water emissivity in the 3.5-4.1 and 8-13 um windows versus zenith angle and wind.  
  *source:* Nalli et al., Developments in ocean infrared emissivity/reflection modeling (AMS, P6.28); Masuda, Takashima, Takayama, Emissivity of pure and sea waters for the model sea surface in the infrared window regions, RSE 24 (1988) — https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/104810.pdf  
  *quote:* "emissivity models are deficient at larger view angles and wind speeds ... R_nu(theta0) ~ eps_nu(theta0) B_nu(Ts) + r_nu(theta0) I_nu_down ... eps_nu(theta0) = 1 - r_nu(theta0)"  
  *for irsim:* A puddle on a road is the sea model applied to a cell: material 'water' with the Fresnel angular emissivity already in materials/angular.py and the reflected sky radiance from SkyModel (ADR 0078/0079/0080). Wire it as a per-cell material override on the ground patch (R1: the puddle is a sub-object region) rather than a new solver. Test that fails if wrong: at 60 degrees off nadir under a clear sky a puddle at the road's kinetic temperature must read several K colder apparent temperature than the adjacent dry asphalt, and within 0.5 K of it at nadir; and the eps + rho = 1 closure (non-negotiable #4) must hold at every angle.
- **Production simulators and wet surfaces (DIRSIG, TAITherm).** DIRSIG's FAQ documents THERM-format and NSRDB weather inputs and 'a significant amount of work ... to address modeling the light from the surface of the water and the volume of water below the surface' (refraction, IOP plugins), but no sentence about rain, wet surfaces, moisture or evaporation in the thermal solver; the Thermal Modality Handbook mentions humidity only as a convection influence. ThermoAnalytics' TAITherm, by contrast, ships a 'Moisture Transport' feature with a moisture-results display for 'evaporation, condensation, and accumulation of moisture on parts'.  
  *source:* DIRSIG FAQ and Thermal Modality Handbook (RIT); TAITherm 2020.1 release notes (ThermoAnalytics support) — https://dirsig.cis.rit.edu/docs/new/faq.html  
  *quote:* "A significant amount of work has been invested in DIRSIG5 to address modeling the light from the surface of the water and the volume of water below the surface."  
  *for irsim:* Positions the wet-film step: DIRSIG (the academic reference) has no evaporation model, the industrial tier (TAITherm/MuSES) has one. irsim's latent term with a per-cell film budget lands between them, and the ADR should say so. It also means Tier 4 comparison of wet scenes must use measured imagery (Hendel-style numbers), not DIRSIG-style synthetic references.

### Snow, vegetation and people

5 findings.

- **Snow: emissivity, black-body treatment, sky correction and the 0 C melt cap (measured alpine dataset).** A high-precision alpine snow radiative-temperature time series (Optris Pi640 microbolometer, 640x480, 8-14 um) treats snow as a black body while noting 'the emissivity of snow is between 0.96 and 0.99 at nadir', ships downwelling longwave measurements so users can correct for emissivity < 1, reaches a mean absolute error of 1.28 K (winter 2021-22) and 0.67 K (spring 2023, T < -1 C) against reference sensors, and shows the snow surface reaching 0 C during melt; the SnowEx tutorial states snow emissivity is 0.98-0.99 'except for coarser grained snow, or snow viewed at a large incidence angle'.  
  *source:* Time series of alpine snow surface radiative-temperature maps from high-precision thermal-infrared imaging, ESSD 16 (2024); SnowEx Hackweek 'Thermal Infrared Remote Sensing of Snow' tutorial — https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/16/3913/2024/  
  *quote:* "Snow has a very high emissivity (0.98-0.99) (except for coarser grained snow, or snow viewed at a large incidence angle ... the emissivity of snow is between 0.96 and 0.99 at nadir"  
  *for irsim:* Snow is a material plus one solver rule. Material: eps_LWIR 0.98-0.99 with the Fresnel-like angular fall-off the library already supports (ECOSTRESS has snow spectra, per the survey). Solver rule (new, in balance.py or a SnowCell wrapper): T_s <= 273.15 K; any surplus net flux at 273.15 K becomes melt at L_f = 334 kJ/kg instead of temperature rise. Tests that fail if wrong: a snow cell under +200 W/m2 net stays at exactly 273.15 K and melts 2.2 mm water-equivalent per hour (200/334000*3600); a clear calm night drives the snow surface several K below air (sky-view longwave deficit) while an overcast night does not; and a public check: irsim's snow scene must reproduce the ESSD dataset's behaviour to within its 0.7-1.3 K MAE.
- **Snow and ice: directional spectral emissivity measurements.** In-situ FTIR measurements of five snow/ice types (fine dendrite, medium granular, coarse grain, sun crust, smooth bare ice) in the 8-14 um window show emissivity decreasing with increasing grain size and observation angle, with the largest spectral contrast at 10.5-12.5 um; Fresnel theory predicts the angular behaviour of smooth bare ice. *(medium confidence)*  
  *source:* Hori et al., In-situ measured spectral directional emissivity of snow and ice in the 8-14 um atmospheric window, Remote Sensing of Environment 100 (2006) 486-502 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425705003974  
  *for irsim:* Justifies giving snow the same angular-emissivity level policy as water (ADR 0042 table layout) with a grain-size parameter, and a per-band note that the 10.5-12.5 um contrast matters for a LWIR band but not MWIR. Not needed for the first snow step; record as the 'revisit when' for a snow material with grain size.
- **Vegetation: leaf energy balance with transpiration (Campbell & Norman form).** The leaf energy balance is phi_N - rho c_p (T_l - T_a) g_H - lambda [rho_sat(T_l) - rho_va] g_W = 0, i.e. net radiation partitioned into sensible heat via the boundary-layer conductance g_H and latent heat via the water-vapour conductance g_W (stomatal in series with boundary layer); Campbell & Norman (1998) give the closed form dT = [(J_a - eps sigma T_a^4 - G)/g_W - lambda drho_va] / {lambda [s + gamma (g_Hr/g_W)]}, and an equivalent textbook form writes Q_a = eps sigma T_l^4 + k1 (V/D)^(1/2) (T_l - T_a) + L(T_l) E with E = [rho_sat(T_l) - rh rho_sat(T_a)]/(r_l + r_a).  
  *source:* BioCro documentation: Energy Balance, Transpiration, and Leaf Temperature (citing Thornley & Johnson 1990 and Campbell & Norman 1998); 'Physical Processes in Ecosystems' ch. 11.3 Leaf Energy Budget — http://biocro.org/BioCro-documentation/latest/pkgdown/articles/web_only/energy_balance.html  
  *quote:* "phi_N - rho c_p ( T_l - T_a ) g_H - lambda [ rho_sat(T_l) - rho_va ] g_W = 0"  
  *for irsim:* Vegetation is the same latent-term balance as the wet film with rs > 0 (stomatal) and a tiny heat capacity (a leaf is a thin panel, well inside balance.py's stability guard). Implement as a ThermalProperties/forcing variant with g_s (or r_s) as a material parameter and VPD from the injected weather; no new solver. Test that fails if wrong: with g_s -> 0 the leaf converges to the dry-reference temperature (above air in sun); with a well-watered g_s it sits below air at high VPD; and the analytic dT formula above must agree with the RK2 steady state to 0.1 K.
- **Vegetation: measured canopy-air temperature differences (CWSI baselines).** Thermal-imaging water-stress work uses a non-transpiring upper reference T_dry = T_air + 5 C and a wet artificial reference for the lower bound, with CWSI = (T_canopy - T_wet)/(T_dry - T_wet); the empirical Idso lower baseline (well-watered canopy minus air vs VPD) has slopes reported between -1.10 and -3.77 C/kPa across locations, i.e. transpiring canopies are several K below air at typical midday VPD.  
  *source:* Jones et al., Use of thermal and visible imagery for estimating crop water status of irrigated grapevine, J. Exp. Bot. 58 (2007); Zhang et al., Maize canopy temperature extracted from UAV thermal and RGB imagery, Frontiers in Plant Science (2019), PMC6794609 — https://academic.oup.com/jxb/article/58/4/827/424148  
  *quote:* "T_dry was estimated by adding 5 C to the measured dry bulb temperature, as suggested by Irmak et al. (2000) ... slopes range from -1.10 to -3.77 C/kPa"  
  *for irsim:* Gives the vegetation cell its Tier 3 numbers: at VPD = 2 kPa a well-watered canopy cell must be 2-7.5 K below air and a stomata-closed cell about 5 K above (in sun). A test that fails if the latent term is missing or mis-scaled: the slope of (T_c - T_a) against VPD from the solver must fall in [-3.8, -1.1] C/kPa for the well-watered parameters. Also relevant to the owner's R1 example (a building part in sun vs shade): a tree canopy next to it is a cold object in daytime LWIR for this reason, and a per-object constant would get the sign wrong.
- **Humans: skin temperature, clothing surface temperature and metabolic heat (ISO 7730 as implemented).** The ISO 7730 PMV implementation computes clothing insulation as icl = 0.155 * clo m2K/W, the clothing area factor f_cl = 1 + 1.29 icl (icl <= 0.078) else 1.05 + 0.645 icl, the convective coefficient as the larger of 2.38 |t_cl - t_a|^0.25 and 12.1 sqrt(v), and the clothing-surface balance whose skin-side term is (308.7 - 0.028 (M - W)) K, i.e. mean skin temperature 35.7 - 0.028 (M - W) C, with radiative exchange 3.96e-8 f_cl (t_cl^4 - t_r^4); ASHRAE 55 defines 1 met = 58.2 W/m2 and comfort skin temperatures of 33-34 C. pythermalcomfort (MIT) also ships the Gagge two-node and JOS-3 multinode models for skin/core temperature and skin wettedness.  
  *source:* pythermalcomfort source _pmv_ppd_optimized.py (Center for the Built Environment, MIT licence) and documentation; ASHRAE 55 (via Wikipedia summary) — https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CenterForTheBuiltEnvironment/pythermalcomfort/master/pythermalcomfort/models/_pmv_ppd_optimized.py  
  *quote:* "icl = 0.155 * clo  # thermal insulation of the clothing in M2K/W ... p5 = (308.7 - 0.028 * mw) + (p2 * (tra / 100.0) ** 4) ... hcn = 2.38 * np.abs(100.0 * xf - taa) ** 0.25 ... hcf = 12.1 * np.sqrt(vr)"  
  *for irsim:* A person is a two-region object (R1 again: face/hands vs clothing are different temperatures on one prim): exposed skin at 35.7 - 0.028 (M - W) C with eps ~ 0.98, and clothing whose surface temperature solves (t_sk - t_cl)/(0.155 I_cl) = f_cl [h_c (t_cl - t_a) + 3.96e-8 f_cl (t_cl^4 - t_r^4)] with t_r from the sky/ground longwave the scene already computes. Implement as irsim/thermal/human.py (engine-free, ~50 lines) and bind two patches per human prim. Tests that fail if wrong: I_cl = 0 gives t_cl = t_sk; at 1 clo and 0 C air the clothing lands roughly 10-15 C below skin; raising wind lowers t_cl; and a golden comparison against pythermalcomfort's two-node model for one condition (MIT, so it may be a dev-only test dependency, but must not be imported by src/irsim).

## Method

Five agents ran in parallel with read-only access to the tree: one audited `src/irsim/thermal`, the scene
schema, the car demo and the point bridge against R1–R3; one audited `docs/roadmap.md` and the queue
machinery; one audited repository health, the spec's coverage of R1–R3 and the README; two ran web
research with the instruction to fetch and read pages rather than trust snippets, and to mark confidence
per finding. A judged multi-proposal design pass was attempted twice and did not complete (it ran out of
session budget both times), so roadmap revision 6 was designed directly from these audits and findings
by the orchestrating session and checked mechanically as it was applied: every row parses, stays under
the 600-character cap, names only existing dependencies, and the queue and phase-table guards pass. The
transcripts are not in the repository; the roadmap's review notes for revision 6 summarise what changed
and why.
