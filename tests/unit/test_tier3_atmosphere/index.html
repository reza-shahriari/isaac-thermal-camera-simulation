Tier3 atmosphere

Tier 3 atmosphere phenomenology and the solar-path stub (M8.8).

tests/unit/test_tier3_atmosphere.py on GitHub

The §7.2 table's decisive claims are not about absolute transmittance -- they are about which band wins: LWIR beats everything in fog, SWIR beats LWIR in humid clear air, and the visible dies first in both. A simulator that reproduces those orderings from the weather alone is doing real work (§7.2); one that merely lands each number in its row could still have the bands in the wrong order. These tests assert the orderings and the crossovers over every committed preset, plus the geometric self-consistency of the layered model and the Bouguer solar stub.

docs/physics-model.md §15 (Tier 3), §7.2, §7.3, §5.4; ADR 0048, 0049, 0051, 0071

test_humid_and_fog_crossover_from_one_preset_and_the_weather		The §7.2 crossover: water vapour hits 8-12 um hardest, so humid clear air makes LWIR worse than SWIR, while fog droplets reverse it. One preset, weather alone.
a target at air temperature is distance invariantparametrize
test_a_target_at_air_temperature_is_distance_invariant		The isothermal identity on a horizontal path: a target radiating exactly L_B(T_air) is indistinguishable from the air at any range, for the grey and the layered model alike. Any error here is a bookkeeping bug in attenuation vs path radiance (§7.1).
short slant path matches the horizontal one
test_short_slant_path_matches_the_horizontal_one		A 300 m path at 10 deg rises only 52 m -- 2.6 % of the water-vapour scale height -- so it must read within 0.1 K of the same length horizontally. This is the self-consistency check on MS.1's column integrals: get the geometry wrong and the two diverge immediately.
bouguer airmass and the 60 degree identity
test_bouguer_airmass_and_the_60_degree_identity		τ_sun(θ) = τ_zenith^{sec θ}: at 60° the airmass is exactly 2, so the transmittance is the zenith value squared. Pinning that identity is what makes the stub checkable before M11 replaces it (ADR 0051).
solar stub orders the presets by how much they block the sun
test_solar_stub_orders_the_presets_by_how_much_they_block_the_sun		Clear air passes more sun than haze, which passes more than fog -- in every band. A preset table that got this backwards would produce daytime MWIR glint in dense fog.
