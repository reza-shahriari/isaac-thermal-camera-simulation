Hemispherical emissivity

M7.8 — the ε a thermal solver needs, which is not the ε a camera sees (§6.1, §6.2, §4.2).

tests/unit/test_hemispherical_emissivity.py on GitHub

A camera measures ε(θ) along one ray; an energy balance radiates into the whole sky. The two differ by more than a rounding — water reads 0.990 at normal and 0.951 hemispherically, because the cos θ sin θ weight peaks at 45° where water's collapse has already begun — and the difference is systematic and one-signed, so it does not average out over a scene.

The sign flips for metals, which is the part that would survive a long time if it were wrong: aluminium's ε rises with angle, so its ε_hemi is above its normal value. Any model that assumed ε_hemi ≤ ε(0) would be right for every dielectric in the library and wrong for every metal.
