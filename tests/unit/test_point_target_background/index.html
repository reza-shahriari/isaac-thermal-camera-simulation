Point target background

AT.22 -- a sub-pixel target subtracts the pixel it sits in, not a clear sky.

tests/unit/test_point_target_background.py on GitHub

excess_radiance under an atmosphere subtracted the clear column beyond R whatever the pixel held. Over a clear-sky pixel that is the same number; over cloud, sea, terrain or another target it is not, and a sub-pixel drone in front of cloud clutter (MS.3) came out about twice as bright over its background as it should be. The fix is the blend rotor_veil already argues for:

ΔL = φ [ τ_B(R) L_t + L_path(R) − L_pixel ].

docs/physics-model.md §5.1, §7.1; ADR 0071, ADR 0081; roadmap MS.6, AT.22.

4 test(s) in tests/unit/test_point_target_background.py.

testwhat it asserts
over a clear sky pixel the two forms agree
test_over_a_clear_sky_pixel_the_two_forms_agree		The pixel-aware form reduces to the per-class clear-column form when the pixel holds the clear column: what every existing scene had, so their frames do not move.
over a cloud pixel the excess is what replaces the pixel
test_over_a_cloud_pixel_the_excess_is_what_replaces_the_pixel		Measured before the fix: over a cloud pixel at 38.66 W/m²/sr with the clear sky at 17.92, a φ = 0.278 target at 700 m was 2× too bright (+5.8 W/m²/sr). Now the excess is the hand value, and it is smaller than the clear-sky excess by φ (L_cloud − L_clear).
the grey atmosphere keeps its own column
test_the_grey_atmosphere_keeps_its_own_column		The grey L1 model's path radiance tends to L_B(T_air) while its sky pixels come from the SkyModel (ADR 0050): not one column, so the pixel cannot say what lies beyond R and the clear-column form stays, whatever the pixel holds (spec issue S28; AT.5).
injection reads the pixel under the target
test_injection_reads_the_pixel_under_the_target		On the plane, the excess over a bright pixel is smaller than over a dark one by exactly φ times the difference, and the frame sum is the excess (flux conserved).
