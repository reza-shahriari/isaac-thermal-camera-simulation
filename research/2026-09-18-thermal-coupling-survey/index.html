The evidence base for roadmap revision 6: the revision that adds the part-to-part, water and fire lanes and makes the existing point-wise machinery reachable from a scene config. Produced by two web-research sweeps and three repository audits run in parallel on 2026-09-18, then judged and adversarially verified; 65 sourced findings (52 high-confidence: page or PDF fetched and read), 48 audit findings with file:line evidence, and 24 open-source codes with licences.

Companion to 2026-09-15-field-survey.md, which it does not repeat: that survey covered MuSES mesh conduction, the DIRSIG THERM limits, the Fraunhofer IOSB prisms, FTOM-2D, VIRSuite and ThRend. This one covers what the owner asked for on 2026-09-18 and the earlier survey did not reach — conduction between parts, the engine as a solved node, hot soak, shadows and sky view from real geometry, the latent-heat term, water bodies, fire and plumes as participating media, and snow, vegetation and people.

This file is the citation list the roadmap's step rows point at. It is a snapshot of what was published and readable on 2026-09-18, not a maintained document: check a source before relying on it, and prefer the primary link over this summary. Numbers quoted here are external evidence, not irsim measurements (risk R13); a roadmap row that adopts one as a tolerance says so.

The thermal core and the Isaac glue#

The engine-free core has real, well-tested pieces of R1 — a PlanarPatch/PlanarThermalField that solves N independent §6.1 cells, an exact ray-rectangle per-cell shadow (shadow.py), and a computed parallel-rectangle view factor (spatial_sources.py) — but none of them is reachable from a scene YAML: patch: blocks exist in the schema and in zero shipped scenes, _build_thermal_field still solves a patched surface as ONE facet, PatchSpec has no occluder field, cell_shadow/patch_solar_loading have no caller outside tests, and the only bound fields (car bonnet, road) are hand-built in Python by car_demo.build_car_demo, which passes no solar term at all. So "a building half in sun from a config plus one command" cannot be produced today; the wall exists only as a unit test, and shadows come from authored ShadowRectangles, never from the object's own mesh. R2 is essentially absent: FacetSolver.net_flux (facets.py:146-158) has no inter-cell term, there is no node-graph/conductance solver anywhere, the engine/exhaust/underbody are independent scripted first-order rows whose coupling to each other is an author-chosen load fraction, and the two conduction-carrying objects that do exist (LumpedTwoNodeSolver: through-thickness only; CabinNode: panel-to-air) have zero callers and no solver: kind. Engine-bay convection onto the bonnet underside is not modelled — it is "absorbed" into a 0.95 cavity emissivity. R3 is thinner still: water is the open-sea skin model only (cool skin + warm layer on an authored bulk SST as a background profile), with no latent-heat/evaporation term in any balance, no consumer of precip_mm_h, wetness as a separate material rather than a state; fire, flame, plume, participating media and gas emission have no code at all (plume is "deferred without a number"; LUT tops out at 1000 K), and the point-target module only offers a grey sub-pixel radiance injection. Structurally, the fixed explicit 1 s/60 s tick with no stability guard and an unbounded tick history (PT.8) cannot host an implicit conduction operator as-is, the PT.6/PT.7 demo constants bypass the material library and start every field uniform, and the per-pixel path is host-side NumPy keyed by instance id + planar patch (curved geometry stays per-prim until the unshipped WM lane). Several docstrings, the CHANGELOG and the roadmap describe PT.2 as making point-wise scenes declarable and consumed by a render driver; no driver reads Scene.patches, so those claims are ahead of the code. For R4 the honest state is: point-wise machinery exists and is correct where tested, but the owner's three headline phenomena are reachable in one Python-authored night car-park scene only, and R2/R3 have no design surface yet.

The roadmap#

The roadmap (docs/roadmap.md, 112 parsed step rows: 84 open, 28 done; queue machinery sound, --check green, every dep id resolves) is a well-built plan for an aerial-then-maritime IR camera, but as written it does not lead to R1-R3. R2 (heat spreading engine -> mounts -> chassis -> panels) has no step at all: the engine is a scripted ΔT-over-ambient schedule (ADR 0089, vehicle.py:1-7), its only coupling to panels is a parallel-rectangle radiative view factor (spatial_sources.py), there is no node-to-node conductance anywhere in the plan, and the nearest steps (PT.11 lateral conduction within one patch, PT.12, PT.15) are all phase C, PT.11 size L at queue position 16 under a phase whose header says "deferred breadth stays deferred". R3 is almost entirely absent: water exists only as the sea, §6.1 has no latent-heat term so evaporation/wet-surface cooling cannot emerge (the spec's own Tier 3 "wet asphalt reads colder" item is unproducible), the weather file's precip_mm_h column is read by nothing, fire and hot gas appear nowhere, and the exhaust-plume deferral's revisit trigger ("MWIR Tier 3") has already been met by the shipped MWIR config and test_tier3_multiband.py without the row changing; snow, vegetation and humans are material YAMLs only. The plan needs roughly five new rows and three re-phasings before it can honestly be said to converge on the owner's camera: a ground per-point binding step that reads Scene.patches plus an occluder block, a building scene config, a thermal-network (node + conductance) step with the engine as a solved node, a latent-heat/wet-surface step, and a numbered plume/fire decision, with PT.11 and the network step pulled out of phase C.

Research findings by topic#

Part-to-part heat transfer: how production codes link parts#

9 findings.

  • (a) heat-source specification in MuSES. MuSES groups geometry into parts (one material/thermal property set per part), places several thermal nodes through each element's thickness, and its user-routine API sets per node any of: temperature, convection coefficient, fluid temperature, imposed heat, capacitance, area, emissivity, absorptivity; it also exposes engine power and speed as scenario inputs and returns per-node conduction/convection/radiation/advection heat rates. Restarts recompute 'view factor and conductor calculations' and require the same number and assignment of thermal nodes. source: Curran & Gonda, Applications of the MuSES Infrared Signature Code, NATO RTO-MP-SCI-145 paper 14 — https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/RTO-MP-SCI-145/MP-SCI-145-14.pdf quote: "Retrieve and Set the temperature, convection coefficient, fluid temperature, imposed heat, capacitance, area, emissivity, or absorptivity of a node." for irsim: R2/R4. irsim.thermal.vehicle and irsim.thermal.aerial only have prescribed-TEMPERATURE nodes. A new engine-free module irsim/thermal/network.py should define a node with capacity C and exactly the three production boundary kinds: fixed temperature, imposed heat Q [W], and convection to a fluid node (h*A, T_fluid), plus conductors G [W/K] between nodes. Engine heat then enters as watts = engine_power * loss_fraction (from VehicleState), not as a scripted skin temperature. Test that would fail if wrong: an imposed-heat node with conductance G to a fixed-temperature sink must settle at dT = Q/G to 1e-6 relative, and switching the same source to a fluid-convection kind must give dT = Q/(hA) - a solver that treats all sources as temperatures cannot pass either.
  • (a) conductor/contactor taxonomy in SINDA/Thermal Desktop. Thermal Desktop defines part-to-part conduction as: edge contact, area contact, node-node conductors on the same or different objects, finite-element conductance, user conductors, and 'contactors' that contact a group of surfaces to another group with a user tolerance and integration level; nodes do not need to be aligned; like conductors are summed before solve. Heat loads apply to surfaces or nodes as flux, time-dependent or area-distributed; heaters take input power and on/off temperatures. RadCAD view factors are Monte Carlo: F_ij = rays from i hitting j / rays shot, 5-20k rays for final runs. source: Introduction to Thermal Desktop, TFAWS 2003 training (C&R Technologies) — https://tfaws.nasa.gov/TFAWS03/software_training/thermal_desktop.pdf quote: "Contact a group of surfaces to another group of surfaces • User controlled tolerance and integration levels ... Nodes do not need to be aligned" for irsim: R2. This is the abstraction PT.11 should adopt instead of only a lateral Laplacian: a Contactor(part_a, part_b, h_c [W/m2K], tolerance_m) that auto-generates conductances between overlapping cells of two PlanarPatch/mesh fields proportional to overlap area, sums duplicates, and needs no grid alignment. Implement in irsim/thermal/network.py with a generator that walks two cell sets. Test: two overlapping patches with h_c=1e4 must give total conductance = h_c * overlap_area within 1 percent, and the same total when one grid is refined 4x (fails if conductance is per node rather than per area).
  • (a) measured contact conductance of a bolted automotive joint. Voller & Tirovic (IJHMT 50, 2007) measured a commercial-vehicle brake disc (grey cast iron) to wheel-carrier (SG iron) joint with ten 12.9 bolts at 300 N m (mean interface pressure 56.2 MPa): local h_cond 2,800-11,400 W/m2K rising with pressure and temperature; average joint conductance 12 kW/m2K new, 7 kW/m2K slightly corroded, 59 kW/m2K with heat-sink paste, 67 kW/m2K with 15 um aluminium foil. source: Voller & Tirovic, Conductive heat transfer across a bolted automotive joint and the influence of interface conditioning, Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer 50 (2007) 4833-4844 — https://sensorprod.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/wp_ijh-2009.pdf quote: "The average hcond values range from 7 to 67 kW/m2 K. The new joint has an average hcond value of 12 kW/m2 K. ... Due to corrosion, thermal conductance is reduced to only 7 kW/m2 K." for irsim: R2. Gives irsim its first sourced joint-conductance table: bolted ferrous automotive joints ~1e4 W/m2K (halve for corrosion). For a 25 cm2 bracket footprint that is ~25 W/K, so a hot manifold or engine conducts strongly into any metal it is bolted to; only rubber breaks the path. Put the values in a configs/ joint table read by network.py (per-area h_c, with corroded/new/paste presets) and cite this paper in the docstring. Test: a two-part model where part B is joined to a 400 C part A through 25 cm2 at 12 kW/m2K vs 1 kW/m2K must show part B's steady rise differ by the conductance ratio against the parallel convective loss (analytic two-resistor check).
  • (a) generic bolted-contact default. A commonly used engineering default for a sturdy dry mechanical interface at moderate pressure such as a bolted connection is ~1,000 W/m2K; a gap filled with a known medium is treated by 1-D Fourier conduction through it. source: DSPE knowledge base, Thermal contact conduction (Thermomechanics ch. 1.2) — https://www.dspe.nl/knowledge/thermomechanics/chapter-1-basics/1-2-heat-transfer/thermal-contact-conduction/ quote: "A commonly used value for a sturdy mechanical interface is around 1000 W/m2K. This would be typical of a dry contact, with a moderate contacting pressure (e.g. a bolted connection)." for irsim: R2. Condensed steam is a droplet cloud, not a gas: FLIR's fire-test note attributes LWIR's ability to image through 'thick layers of smoke and steam' to wavelengths larger than the steam droplets, while the MWIR camera needed a narrowband filter and still lost the scene; NIRATAM shows the gaseous H2O/CO2 of a plume transmits 90-100 % in 8-10 um, so what a LWIR camera sees of a steam plume is the droplet phase (emitting near the droplet temperature, cooling from ~100 C at the exit to ambient) and it fades as the droplets evaporate. source: Application of Mid-Wave and Long-Wave IR Imaging in Large-Scale Rack-Storage Fires (Teledyne FLIR) and Bakker et al. 1999 (TNO/NIRATAM) — https://www.infraredtraining.com/en-US/home/resources/blog/application-of-mid-wave-and-long-wave-ir-imaging--in-large-scale-rack-storage-fires/ quote: "clearly showing the fire location and suppression behavior of the water through thick layers of smoke and steam ... Long-wave infrared utilizes wavelengths larger than the size of steam droplets or soot particles, resulted in great signal transmittance" for irsim: Steam/vapour is the same slab operator as fire but with a droplet extinction (Mie, scaled by liquid water content, as irsim/atmosphere/cloud.py and extinction.py already do for fog/cloud) rather than a gas kappa_b(T), emitting at the droplet temperature. Tests that fail if the physics is wrong: for the same LWC the MWIR extinction must exceed the LWIR extinction (band ordering), the apparent temperature of the plume must never exceed the authored droplet temperature, and a pure-gas H2O slab at 373 K must be nearly invisible in LWIR (tau >= 0.9 per NIRATAM). Rotor-veil injection (rotor_veil.py) is the existing precedent for a translucent overlay in the pipeline.

Fire on a real camera: gain states, saturation and AGC#

2 findings.

  • Fire on a LWIR microbolometer: gain states, saturation, AGC. The FLIR Boson provides high-gain and low-gain states: in high gain 'hot objects in the scene are prone to saturation' while in low gain 'scene range is significantly higher'; intrascene temperature is the span that maps to the 16-bit output without railing, and radiometric cameras' output is not capped; the AGC is a variant of histogram equalisation in which 'an image with 60 % sky will devote 60 % of the available 8-bit values ... to the sky', with plateau, tail-rejection, max-gain and linear-percent parameters. FLIR product pages give the ranges as up to 140 C (high gain) and 500 C (low gain). source: FLIR Boson Thermal Imaging Core datasheet, Doc. 102-2013-40 Rev 340 (March 2021), sections 6.5, 6.8, 11.2 — https://groupgets-files.s3.amazonaws.com/boson/documents/Boson%20datasheet,%20102-2013-40,%20Rev%20340.pdf quote: "In high-gain state, the thermal sensitivity is highest (i.e., NEDT is lowest), but hot objects in the scene are prone to saturation. In low-gain state, the thermal sensitivity is lower, but scene range is significantly higher ... In classic HEQ, an image with 60% sky will devote 60% of the available 8-bit values (referred to as grayshades here forward) to the sky" for irsim: The camera the project validates against (configs/sensors/halmstad_boson_320.yaml) needs a gain_state with an intrascene ceiling so a fire rails the 16-bit signal in high gain and merely compresses it in low gain; the bolometer.py 'un-clipped signal' must be clipped at the ceiling before the ISP. Test in the test_agc_collapse.py family: a 1000 K slab covering 2 % of pixels over a 300 K scene must, under agc_plateau, leave the background at least N grayshades (plateau-limited), whereas agc_linear collapses it - and a radiometric config must keep the true temperature above the 8-bit ceiling (FLAME 3's 500 C cap is the reference behaviour).
  • Fire on a LWIR camera: linear AGC collapse quantified (FLIR). FLIR's own AGC note: with a 37 C human in a 30 C room and a 1000 C fire, under linear AGC 'the 7 degrees Celsius between the human and the room won't show in the image as visible contrast, since it represents only 0.7% of the total range'; capping the range at 100 C makes 'everything hotter than 100 C look the same'; histogram equalisation (CLAHE in the example) recovers both. source: Basic AGC for radiometric Lepton images, Teledyne FLIR OEM — https://oem.flir.com/learn/discover/basic-agc-for-radiometric-lepton-images/ quote: "the 7 degrees Celsius between the human and the room won't show in the image as visible contrast, since it represents only 0.7% of the total range" for irsim: A numeric acceptance test for irsim.isp.agc under fire: render (or synthesise via conftest fixtures) a frame with a 303 K background, a 310 K person and a 1273 K fire patch; assert agc_linear yields < 2 DN person/room contrast (0.7 % of 255) and agc_plateau yields >= 10 DN. Also a reminder for R4 ('a real infrared camera'): the delivered 8-bit frame of a fire scene is dominated by the AGC choice, so the config must record which AGC mode the reference camera used.

Water bodies, wet surfaces and evaporation#

9 findings.

  • (R3) wet surfaces and evaporative cooling. Hendel's cool-pavement review formalises the surface balance with a latent term (evaporation rate x latent heat) for a water film whose evaporation rate rises with film temperature and depends on ambient vapour pressure; reviews of pavement watering report surface-temperature reductions of 10-15 C during insolation (Hendel et al. 2014, 2016) and up to ~40 C for water-retaining pavements, detected by infrared camera as temperature spikes when the surface dries. (medium confidence) source: Hendel, Cool pavements (arXiv 2409.12242); Thermal performance of cooling strategies for asphalt pavement: a state-of-the-art review — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12242 quote: "the evaporation rate increases with the water film temperature" for irsim: R3. Add a water-film state and latent flux E*l to irsim.thermal.balance (FacetProperties gains wetness_kg_m2; forcing gains vapour pressure from WeatherSample humidity, which WeatherSeries already carries per non-negotiable #6). Test: a wetted asphalt facet at solar noon is >=10 K cooler than the dry twin and rejoins it once the film mass reaches zero (fails if latent term or film depletion is missing).
  • Wet surfaces: the latent-heat term (Penman-Monteith, FAO-56). The Penman-Monteith combination equation gives latent heat flux from net radiation Rn, soil heat flux G, vapour pressure deficit (es - ea), air density and cp, the saturation-curve slope, the psychrometric constant and the bulk surface and aerodynamic resistances rs and ra; ra = ln((zm - d)/zom) ln((zh - d)/zoh) / (k^2 uz) with von Karman k = 0.41; the latent heat of vaporisation used is 2.45 MJ/kg. source: FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56, Chapter 2: FAO Penman-Monteith equation — https://www.fao.org/4/x0490e/x0490e06.htm quote: "where Rn is the net radiation, G is the soil heat flux, (es - ea) represents the vapour pressure deficit of the air, ra is the mean air density at constant pressure, cp is the specific heat of the air, D represents the slope of the saturation vapour pressure temperature relationship, g is the psychrometric constant, and rs and ra are the (bulk) surface and aerodynamic resistances" for irsim: The missing term in balance.py's sec. 6.1 equation is -lambda E. For a free water film rs = 0 and Penman-Monteith reduces to the bulk form E = rho_a (q_sat(T_s) - q_a)/ra, so one implementation covers wet pavement (rs = 0), vegetation (rs = stomatal) and bare soil (rs from moisture). q_sat comes from irsim.atmosphere.humidity (vapour_pressure_hpa already exists), lambda = 2.45e6 J/kg goes into irsim.radiometry.constants with this citation. Test that fails if wrong: a saturated cell with no radiation and h > 0 relaxes to the psychrometric wet-bulb temperature of (T_air, RH), and its steady temperature decreases monotonically with decreasing RH; a dry cell (film mass 0) must reproduce today's result bit-for-bit.
  • Wet surfaces / water bodies: bulk aerodynamic flux formulae (AeroBulk / COARE). AeroBulk implements the bulk formulae Q_H = rho C_H c_p (theta_z - T_s) U_B and E = rho C_E (q_s - q_z) U_B with Q_L = -L_v E, offering COARE v3.0/v3.6, ECMWF, ANDREAS and NCAR algorithms, with a cool-skin/warm-layer scheme available when the input is bulk SST; licence GPL-3.0. source: AeroBulk: aerodynamic bulk formulae for air-sea turbulent fluxes (GitHub README) — https://github.com/brodeau/aerobulk quote: "In the COARE and ECMWF algorithms, a cool-skin/warm layer scheme is included and can be activated if the input sea-surface temperature to be used is the bulk SST." for irsim: sea_skin.py's docstring already admits the latent term is 'usually the largest of the three' and is missing. Add E = rho C_E U (q_sat(T_skin) - q_a) with C_E ~ C_H (Dalton number) to the sea and lake skin balance and to the wet-film cell; use AeroBulk (GPL, so oracle only, never vendored) to pin a golden value, e.g. Q_L for U = 5 m/s, T_s = 293 K, RH = 0.7 must land within 15 % of COARE 3.6. Because irsim's WeatherSample carries rh_fraction and wind, no schema change is needed; a test must assert that removing the latent term warms the sea skin (sign check) and that Q_L = 0 when RH = 1 and T_s = T_a.
  • Wet surfaces: how EnergyPlus models rain (the production shortcut). EnergyPlus: 'When the outside environment indicates that it is raining, the exterior surfaces (exposed to wind) are assumed to be wet. The convection coefficient is set to a very high number (1000) and the outside temperature used for the surface will be the wet-bulb temperature.' No evaporation or latent heat is modelled on opaque exterior surfaces. source: EnergyPlus 25.1 Engineering Reference: Outside Surface Heat Balance — https://bigladdersoftware.com/epx/docs/25-1/engineering-reference/outside-surface-heat-balance.html quote: "When the outside environment indicates that it is raining, the exterior surfaces (exposed to wind) are assumed to be wet. The convection coefficient is set to a very high number (1000)" for irsim: A one-commit ablation level for wet surfaces before the full film model (ADR 0083 pattern: fidelity switches are config): while precip_mm_h > 0, set h = 1000 W/m2/K and T_air := T_wb in FacetForcing. It is also a correctness check on the later latent term: with rs = 0 and large h the full model must converge to the same wet-bulb clamp. Test: rain on a facet gives |T_s - T_wb(T_air, RH)| < 0.1 K within one tick; and the WeatherSeries used by the atmosphere is the same object (non-negotiable #6), so a test asserts that the precip flag comes from the injected series, not a scene flag.
  • Wet surfaces: measured wet-vs-dry asphalt temperature difference and drying time. Pavement watering in Paris (0.20 mm per application every 60 min in shade and every 30 min in sun, FLIR B400 camera) reduced asphalt surface temperature by up to 4 C during shading and 13 C during insolation; averaged over zones, 2-4 C in the morning and 6-13 C in the afternoon; the camera detected 'temperature spikes indicative of surface drying', i.e. the surface re-warms abruptly when the film is gone within the 30-60 min cycle. source: Hendel, Colombert, Diab, Royon, Improving a pavement-watering method on the basis of pavement surface temperature measurements, Urban Climate (2014), arXiv 1412.2165 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.2165 quote: "surface temperature reductions of up to 4 C during shading and 13 C during insolation were found. Secondly, the infrared camera successfully detected temperature spikes indicative of surface drying" for irsim: The Tier 3 phenomenology numbers for the wet-film cell: an asphalt PlanarPatch at noon, clear summer weather (existing clear_midlat_summer_48h), with a 0.2 mm film on half its cells must render the wet half 6-13 K colder than the dry half and 2-4 K in shade, and the film must evaporate in roughly 30-60 min (mass budget E dt vs 0.2 kg/m2) after which the two halves reconverge - the 'spike'. This is R1 in its purest form: wet and dry are per-cell states of one object, so the film mass must live on the cell like q_solar, not on the prim. A test that fails if the latent term is scaled wrong: drying time outside 15-120 min at the paper's conditions.
  • Wet surfaces: asphalt thermal behaviour dry vs saturated (Nottingham) and permeable-pavement cooling. Hassn, Chiarelli, Dawson and Garcia (2016) exposed dry and water-saturated asphalt slabs to infrared light and measured surface/bottom temperature, heat flux and evaporation rate: under wet conditions 'the energy used by water for evaporation reduces drastically the temperature of asphalt mixture', whereas in dry conditions air voids act only through heat capacity and conductivity; related permeable-pavement studies report 5-15 C lower surface temperatures with evaporative cooling. (medium confidence) source: Thermal properties of asphalt pavements under dry and wet conditions, Materials & Design (2016), Nottingham ePrints 35660; Li et al., Cooling effect of permeable asphalt pavement under dry and wet conditions — https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/35660/ for irsim: Second, independent magnitude (5-15 C) for the wet-asphalt test above; also a reminder that water in the pores changes the slab's k and rho c, so the wet state should raise the cell's heat_capacity_j_m2_k as well as add evaporation. Keep it as a documented tolerance, not a golden number, until the paper is read.
  • Inland water bodies: skin vs bulk temperature. At Lake Tahoe the night-time skin effect had a mean of 0.3-0.5 K (skin cooler than bulk) and the daytime effect a mean of ~0.5 K in the morning falling to 0.1 K by midday; increased downwelling sky radiation from cloud or high humidity produced night-time skin temperatures warmer than bulk. Microprofiles on a stratified lake at 0.8-8.2 m/s winds show the diffusive thermal sublayer is 0.7-3.6 mm thick and natural convection sets its thickness and the skin-to-bulk difference. (medium confidence) source: Wilson et al., Skin and bulk temperature difference at Lake Tahoe: a case study on lake skin effect, JGR Atmospheres (2013); Hondzo et al., Measurement and Scaling of Lake Surface Skin Temperatures, GRL (2022) — https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jgrd.50786 quote: "The diffusive thermal sublayer thickness ranged from 0.7 to 3.6 mm. The temperature microprofiles and proposed parameterization of skin-to-bulk temperatures enable similarity scaling" for irsim: Lakes and rivers reuse sea_skin.py (cool_skin_thickness_m, cool_skin_deficit_k, warm_layer_k) with fresh-water constants and a much larger effective heat capacity than any solid cell, so their diurnal amplitude is small and lagged. Two tests that fail if the physics is wrong: (a) clear calm night gives skin - bulk in [-0.5, -0.1] K; (b) cloudy humid night (Q_LW_down high) flips the sign to skin warmer than bulk - a model that only ever cools the skin fails (b). The computed sublayer thickness at 0.8-8.2 m/s must lie in 0.7-3.6 mm.
  • Standing water and puddles: angular emissivity and reflected sky. Surface-leaving radiance over water is R_nu(theta) = eps_nu(theta) B_nu(T_s) + r_nu(theta) I_nu_down with eps = 1 - r; the reflectivity comes from the Fresnel equations for a specular surface plus a wave-slope distribution; field spectra (M-AERI) show that the classic emissivity models 'are deficient at larger view angles and wind speeds', and the cross-over angle where roughness starts to raise emissivity is 30-40 degrees rather than the 68 degrees of Masuda et al. (1988), whose model tabulates water emissivity in the 3.5-4.1 and 8-13 um windows versus zenith angle and wind. source: Nalli et al., Developments in ocean infrared emissivity/reflection modeling (AMS, P6.28); Masuda, Takashima, Takayama, Emissivity of pure and sea waters for the model sea surface in the infrared window regions, RSE 24 (1988) — https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/104810.pdf quote: "emissivity models are deficient at larger view angles and wind speeds ... R_nu(theta0) ~ eps_nu(theta0) B_nu(Ts) + r_nu(theta0) I_nu_down ... eps_nu(theta0) = 1 - r_nu(theta0)" for irsim: A puddle on a road is the sea model applied to a cell: material 'water' with the Fresnel angular emissivity already in materials/angular.py and the reflected sky radiance from SkyModel (ADR 0078/0079/0080). Wire it as a per-cell material override on the ground patch (R1: the puddle is a sub-object region) rather than a new solver. Test that fails if wrong: at 60 degrees off nadir under a clear sky a puddle at the road's kinetic temperature must read several K colder apparent temperature than the adjacent dry asphalt, and within 0.5 K of it at nadir; and the eps + rho = 1 closure (non-negotiable #4) must hold at every angle.
  • Production simulators and wet surfaces (DIRSIG, TAITherm). DIRSIG's FAQ documents THERM-format and NSRDB weather inputs and 'a significant amount of work ... to address modeling the light from the surface of the water and the volume of water below the surface' (refraction, IOP plugins), but no sentence about rain, wet surfaces, moisture or evaporation in the thermal solver; the Thermal Modality Handbook mentions humidity only as a convection influence. ThermoAnalytics' TAITherm, by contrast, ships a 'Moisture Transport' feature with a moisture-results display for 'evaporation, condensation, and accumulation of moisture on parts'. source: DIRSIG FAQ and Thermal Modality Handbook (RIT); TAITherm 2020.1 release notes (ThermoAnalytics support) — https://dirsig.cis.rit.edu/docs/new/faq.html quote: "A significant amount of work has been invested in DIRSIG5 to address modeling the light from the surface of the water and the volume of water below the surface." for irsim: Positions the wet-film step: DIRSIG (the academic reference) has no evaporation model, the industrial tier (TAITherm/MuSES) has one. irsim's latent term with a per-cell film budget lands between them, and the ADR should say so. Snow, vegetation and people#

5 findings.

  • Snow: emissivity, black-body treatment, sky correction and the 0 C melt cap (measured alpine dataset). A high-precision alpine snow radiative-temperature time series (Optris Pi640 microbolometer, 640x480, 8-14 um) treats snow as a black body while noting 'the emissivity of snow is between 0.96 and 0.99 at nadir', ships downwelling longwave measurements so users can correct for emissivity < 1, reaches a mean absolute error of 1.28 K (winter 2021-22) and 0.67 K (spring 2023, T < -1 C) against reference sensors, and shows the snow surface reaching 0 C during melt; the SnowEx tutorial states snow emissivity is 0.98-0.99 'except for coarser grained snow, or snow viewed at a large incidence angle'. source: Time series of alpine snow surface radiative-temperature maps from high-precision thermal-infrared imaging, ESSD 16 (2024); SnowEx Hackweek 'Thermal Infrared Remote Sensing of Snow' tutorial — https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/16/3913/2024/ quote: "Snow has a very high emissivity (0.98-0.99) (except for coarser grained snow, or snow viewed at a large incidence angle ... the emissivity of snow is between 0.96 and 0.99 at nadir" for irsim: Snow is a material plus one solver rule.   • Snow and ice: directional spectral emissivity measurements. (medium confidence) source: Hori et al., In-situ measured spectral directional emissivity of snow and ice in the 8-14 um atmospheric window, Remote Sensing of Environment 100 (2006) 486-502 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425705003974 for irsim: Justifies giving snow the same angular-emissivity level policy as water (ADR 0042 table layout) with a grain-size parameter, and a per-band note that the 10.5-12.5 um contrast matters for a LWIR band but not MWIR.   • Vegetation: leaf energy balance with transpiration (Campbell & Norman form). The leaf energy balance is phi_N - rho c_p (T_l - T_a) g_H - lambda [rho_sat(T_l) - rho_va] g_W = 0, i.e. net radiation partitioned into sensible heat via the boundary-layer conductance g_H and latent heat via the water-vapour conductance g_W (stomatal in series with boundary layer); Campbell & Norman (1998) give the closed form dT = [(J_a - eps sigma T_a^4 - G)/g_W - lambda drho_va] / {lambda [s + gamma (g_Hr/g_W)]}, and an equivalent textbook form writes Q_a = eps sigma T_l^4 + k1 (V/D)^(1/2) (T_l - T_a) + L(T_l) E with E = [rho_sat(T_l) - rh rho_sat(T_a)]/(r_l + r_a). source: BioCro documentation: Energy Balance, Transpiration, and Leaf Temperature (citing Thornley & Johnson 1990 and Campbell & Norman 1998); 'Physical Processes in Ecosystems' ch. 11.3 Leaf Energy Budget — http://biocro.org/BioCro-documentation/latest/pkgdown/articles/web_only/energy_balance.html quote: "phi_N - rho c_p ( T_l - T_a ) g_H - lambda [ rho_sat(T_l) - rho_va ] g_W = 0" for irsim: Vegetation is the same latent-term balance as the wet film with rs > 0 (stomatal) and a tiny heat capacity (a leaf is a thin panel, well inside balance.py's stability guard). Implement as a ThermalProperties/forcing variant with g_s (or r_s) as a material parameter and VPD from the injected weather; no new solver. Test that fails if wrong: with g_s -> 0 the leaf converges to the dry-reference temperature (above air in sun); with a well-watered g_s it sits below air at high VPD; and the analytic dT formula above must agree with the RK2 steady state to 0.1 K.
  • Vegetation: measured canopy-air temperature differences (CWSI baselines). Thermal-imaging water-stress work uses a non-transpiring upper reference T_dry = T_air + 5 C and a wet artificial reference for the lower bound, with CWSI = (T_canopy - T_wet)/(T_dry - T_wet); the empirical Idso lower baseline (well-watered canopy minus air vs VPD) has slopes reported between -1.10 and -3.77 C/kPa across locations, i.e. transpiring canopies are several K below air at typical midday VPD. source: Jones et al., Use of thermal and visible imagery for estimating crop water status of irrigated grapevine, J. Exp. Bot. 58 (2007); Zhang et al., Maize canopy temperature extracted from UAV thermal and RGB imagery, Frontiers in Plant Science (2019), PMC6794609 — https://academic.oup.com/jxb/article/58/4/827/424148 quote: "T_dry was estimated by adding 5 C to the measured dry bulb temperature, as suggested by Irmak et al. Method#

Five agents ran in parallel with read-only access to the tree: one audited src/irsim/thermal, the scene schema, the car demo and the point bridge against R1–R3; one audited docs/roadmap.md and the queue machinery; one audited repository health, the spec's coverage of R1–R3 and the README; two ran web research with the instruction to fetch and read pages rather than trust snippets, and to mark confidence per finding. A judged multi-proposal design pass was attempted twice and did not complete (it ran out of session budget both times), so roadmap revision 6 was designed directly from these audits and findings by the orchestrating session and checked mechanically as it was applied: every row parses, stays under the 600-character cap, names only existing dependencies, and the queue and phase-table guards pass. The transcripts are not in the repository; the roadmap's review notes for revision 6 summarise what changed and why.
